---
title: Understanding derivations and proofs
tags: maths, derivation, proof, theorem, formula, equation, lemma, calculus, linear-algebra, notation
summary: For slides that derive a result or prove a theorem: ask why each step is allowed, what each symbol means, and where the assumptions are used.
---
Use when a slide walks through a derivation, proof, theorem or a formula built from earlier ones.

Question moves:
- Justify a step: "Why can we go from <line> to <line> here? Which rule or assumption allows it?"
- Name the assumption: "Where in this proof is <condition> actually used? What breaks without it?"
- Read the formula: "What does each term in <formula> represent, and what happens to it when <variable> grows?"
- Intuition before algebra: "What's the intuition behind <result>, before the algebra?"
- Special cases: "What does <formula> reduce to when <parameter> is 0 or 1, and does that make sense?"
- Connect: "How does this result depend on <earlier theorem / definition>?"

Good: "Why does dividing by n-1 instead of n make the sample variance unbiased?"
Weak: "Explain this proof." / "What is the formula?"

Watch for: notation that changes meaning between slides, skipped algebra, and conditions stated once in small print.
